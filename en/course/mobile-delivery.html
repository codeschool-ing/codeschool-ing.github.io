Publishing and Operating a Mobile App

From the signed build to the version already on people’s phones: stores, review, staged rollouts, crash reports, and the metric that says whether anybody came back.

  • 60 hours
  • Level: intermediate

What you need first

Requires a working app and a pipeline. It does not teach how to build one — it covers everything that happens after the build succeeds.

What you will learn

  • Signing, certificates and the keys you must not lose
  • The stores: listing, review, and the rejection that repeats
  • Releasing in stages, and the rollback that does not exist
  • Crashes: symbols, stack traces and the bug only release has
  • Analytics, funnels and retention on mobile
  • Living with versions in the wild: forced updates and deprecation

Full topic list

  • Signing on Android: keystore, Play App Signing, and the key that cannot be lost
  • Signing on iOS: certificates, provisioning profiles, and the expiry that stops the build
  • Play Console and App Store Connect: accounts, roles and fees
  • The store listing: screenshots, description, keywords and assets
  • Privacy labels, data declarations, and the permission you have to justify
  • Review: the common rejections, the appeal, and how long it takes
  • Internal, closed and open testing; TestFlight
  • Staged rollout, and halting a release that is crashing
  • Versioning: version name, build number, and the semantics people read
  • CI for mobile: a hosted Mac, caching, and signing inside the pipeline
  • Fastlane: build, screenshots, metadata and upload
  • Crash reporting with Crashlytics or Sentry, and uploading symbols
  • Reading a stack trace from a minified release build
  • ANRs, watchdog kills, and the crash that is not a crash
  • Performance in the field: start-up time, dropped frames and battery
  • Analytics: events, funnels, and measurement that is not surveillance
  • Retention, DAU/MAU, and the churn a mobile app really has
  • App size, install conversion, and what the store shows before the download
  • Forced update, remote config and feature flags
  • Supporting versions in the wild: the API contract and the app that never updates
