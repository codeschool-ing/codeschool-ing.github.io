Course

Statistics for Data Analysis

The foundation that separates reading a number from understanding it: describe, compare, test and never confuse correlation with cause.

  • 80 hours
  • Level: beginner

What you need first

No programming required. It comes before Python on the BI track, on purpose.

What you will learn

  • Variables and data types
  • Descriptive statistics: centre, spread and shape
  • Distributions and what they reveal
  • Inferential statistics: from sample to population
  • Correlation, causality and regression
  • Hypothesis tests, p-values and confidence intervals

Full topic list

  • Categorical and numerical variables
  • Discrete and continuous variables; nominal, ordinal, interval and ratio scales
  • Measures of central tendency: mean, median and mode
  • When the mean lies and the median saves you
  • Measures of spread: range, variance and standard deviation
  • Quartiles, interquartile range (IQR) and the boxplot
  • The shape of a distribution: skewness and kurtosis
  • Normal, uniform, binomial and Poisson distributions
  • Outliers: detecting them, understanding them and deciding
  • Population and sample; random, stratified and convenience sampling
  • The central limit theorem in plain language
  • Standard error and confidence intervals
  • Hypothesis testing: null hypothesis, alternative and significance level
  • The p-value: what it says and what it does not
  • Type I and type II errors; statistical power
  • Common tests: t-test, chi-square, ANOVA and non-parametric tests
  • Pearson and Spearman correlation
  • Correlation is not causation: confounders and reverse causality
  • Simple and multiple linear regression: coefficients and R²
  • Residual diagnostics and the assumptions of regression
  • Beyond linear regression: logistic regression and an overview of models
  • Statistical significance versus practical relevance
