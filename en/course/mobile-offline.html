Data on the Device: Offline, Sync and Conflicts

The network is optional and the app is not. Local storage, an offline-first architecture, a synchronisation that resumes, and the conflict two devices will always eventually create.

  • 60 hours
  • Level: intermediate

What you need first

Requires an app that already reads from an API — this is about what happens when that API is not there. Any of the three platforms: the examples come from all of them.

What you will learn

  • What lives on the device, and the sandbox it lives in
  • Local databases, and the query that keeps the interface fast
  • Offline-first: the local write as the source of truth
  • Synchronisation: queue, retry, idempotency and deltas
  • Conflicts: last write wins, versioning and CRDTs
  • Encryption on the device, and the data that should not be there

Full topic list

  • The app sandbox: files, preferences, caches, and what the backup takes
  • Key-value storage: preferences, MMKV and UserDefaults
  • Local databases: SQLite, Room, SwiftData and Realm
  • Modelling for a device: normalise less, read faster
  • Migrations on a device you cannot log into
  • Reading on a background thread, and the list that stutters
  • Offline-first: write locally first, tell the user afterwards
  • The outbox: a queue of operations waiting for the network
  • Retry, exponential backoff, and the request that must not repeat
  • Idempotency keys, and the payment that was sent twice
  • Delta sync: cursors, timestamps, and the clock that is wrong
  • Full resync: when starting again is the cheaper option
  • Conflicts: last write wins, and what it silently throws away
  • Version numbers, vector clocks and merging field by field
  • CRDTs, and the libraries that implement them
  • Background sync: what Android and iOS allow, and what they kill
  • Cache: what expires, what is pinned, and how large it may grow
  • Images and files offline, and the disk that fills up
  • Encryption at rest, the keystore and biometric unlock
  • Data that should never be on the device, and what the store makes you declare
