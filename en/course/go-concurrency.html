Course

Go: Concurrency and Testing

The part of Go that does not exist in other languages: thousands of tasks at once, with no framework and no fright.

  • 70 hours
  • Level: intermediate

What you need first

Requires the Go course: syntax, interfaces and errors are assumed here.

What you will learn

  • Goroutines and channels: cheap concurrency
  • Synchronisation with `sync`, mutexes and wait groups
  • Cancellation and deadlines with the `context` package
  • Patterns: fan-in, fan-out, pipeline and worker pool
  • Race condition detection
  • The standard library and real testing: table-driven tests, mocks and benchmarks

Full topic list

  • Concurrency is not parallelism: what Go actually promises
  • Goroutines: cost, scheduler and what happens in `main`
  • Channels: sending, receiving, closing and ranging
  • Buffered and unbuffered channels: the choice that causes deadlock
  • `select`, timeouts and the `default` case
  • Goroutine leaks: how they happen and how to avoid them
  • Worker pool: limiting concurrency on purpose
  • The `sync` package: `Mutex`, `RWMutex` and when a channel is worse
  • `WaitGroup`, `Once` and `sync.Map`
  • The `context` package: cancellation, deadlines and values
  • Propagating context through the whole call: the first-parameter convention
  • Use cases: HTTP request, database query and shutdown
  • Fan-out pattern: spreading work across goroutines
  • Fan-in pattern: merging several channels into one
  • Pipeline pattern: chained stages with correct closing
  • The race detector (`-race`): running it, reading the output and fixing it
  • Input and output: `io.Reader`, `io.Writer` and composing them
  • Files and directories with `os`, efficient reading with `bufio`
  • `encoding/json`: marshal, unmarshal, tags and optional fields
  • `time`: durations, timers, tickers and time zones
  • `flag` for command-line arguments
  • `slog`: structured logging in the standard library
  • `regexp` and the cost of a regular expression
  • `go:embed`: bundling files into the binary
  • The `testing` package: your first test, `t.Run` and subtests
  • Table-driven tests: the standard Go idiom
  • Mocks, stubs and the small interface that makes them easy
  • `httptest` for testing HTTP servers and clients
  • Benchmarks: writing them, reading the result and not fooling yourself
  • Test coverage: measuring it and knowing what it does not tell you
