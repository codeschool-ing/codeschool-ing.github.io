Network Security

Defending the network properly: a well-configured firewall, cryptography you actually understand, known attacks and least-privilege access.

  • 70 hours
  • Level: intermediate

What you need first

Requires networks: you cannot defend a topology you cannot draw.

What you will learn

  • Firewalls, proxies and the DMZ
  • Common attacks and how each one works
  • Cryptography fundamentals and certificates
  • Intrusion detection and prevention (IDS/IPS)
  • Access control lists (ACLs)
  • Zero Trust architecture and network access control

Full topic list

  • Stateful and stateless firewalls
  • Next-generation firewall (NGFW) and deep packet inspection
  • Web application firewall (WAF) and reverse proxy
  • DMZ, segmentation and designing trust zones
  • Writing and ordering rules: deny by default
  • Denial of service (DoS and DDoS) and mitigation
  • Sniffing, ARP spoofing and taking a session over on the wire
  • Address spoofing, ARP poisoning and DNS poisoning
  • Phishing, social engineering and ransomware on the corporate network
  • Symmetric and asymmetric cryptography: where each one fits
  • Hash functions, digital signatures and integrity
  • Public key infrastructure (PKI), certificates and the chain of trust
  • TLS on the wire: downgrade, stripping and a certificate nobody validated
  • IDS and IPS: detecting versus blocking
  • Snort and Suricata: signatures and anomaly detection
  • NIDS and HIDS: a sensor on the network and a sensor on the host
  • Standard and extended ACLs: filtering by source, destination, port and protocol
  • Rule evaluation order and the classic mistakes
  • Least privilege applied to the network
  • Zero Trust: never trust, always verify
  • Microsegmentation and continuous identity verification
  • Network access control (NAC) and 802.1X authentication
  • Network logs: flow records, IDS alerts and what is worth keeping
