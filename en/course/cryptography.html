Applied Cryptography

Without understanding cryptography you are only repeating configuration from a tutorial. Here you learn what each option protects.

  • 50 hours
  • Level: intermediate

What you need first

Requires security fundamentals. The mathematics stays at the level needed to choose, not to prove.

What you will learn

  • Symmetric and asymmetric cryptography
  • Hashing, salting and password storage
  • Key exchange and public key infrastructure
  • Certificates, the chain of trust and TLS in practice
  • Secure protocols and their insecure equivalents
  • Cryptography on wireless networks

Full topic list

  • Symmetric cryptography: AES, modes of operation and the initialisation vector
  • Asymmetric cryptography: RSA, elliptic curves and key size
  • Public and private keys: who encrypts and who verifies
  • Hash functions: MD5, SHA-1, SHA-2 and why the first two fell
  • Salting, peppering and key derivation (bcrypt, Argon2)
  • Digital signatures and message integrity (HMAC)
  • Key exchange: Diffie-Hellman and forward secrecy
  • Public key infrastructure (PKI) and certificate authorities
  • Certificates: fields, validity, revocation and the chain of trust
  • The TLS handshake step by step and what can go wrong
  • Obfuscation and encoding: why neither is encryption
  • Protocols: FTP and SFTP, SSL and TLS, LDAP and LDAPS
  • IPsec, DNSSEC, SRTP and S/MIME
  • Disk and database encryption
  • Wi-Fi: WEP, WPA, WPA2 and WPA3
  • EAP, PEAP and authentication on the corporate network
  • Classic mistakes: keys in the code, rolling your own algorithm and nonce reuse
