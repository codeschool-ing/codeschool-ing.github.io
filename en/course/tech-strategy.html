Technical Strategy: Debt, Budget and Build versus Buy

Deciding where the engineering money goes: technical debt as a budget line, buying instead of building, and a plan that survives contact with the quarter.

  • 50 hours
  • Level: advanced

What you need first

Requires experience of a system in production and of a team’s week. The examples assume you have seen a migration, a rewrite proposal and a cloud bill.

What you will learn

  • What a technical strategy is, and why a roadmap is not one
  • Technical debt: the deliberate kind, the accidental kind, and how to price it
  • Build, buy or adopt: the decision and its cost over three years
  • The budget: cloud, licences, headcount, and what cutting means
  • Platform and paved roads: standardising without policing
  • Aligning with product, and saying no with a reason attached

Full topic list

  • A strategy is a diagnosis, a guiding policy and coherent action — not a list of goals
  • Vision, strategy, roadmap and backlog: four different documents
  • Writing the one page the team can repeat back to you
  • Technical debt: deliberate and accidental, prudent and reckless
  • Pricing debt as interest paid per sprint, not as a number in a spreadsheet
  • The rewrite that is never worth it, and the two cases where it is
  • The strangler pattern: migrating while production keeps running
  • Build, buy, or adopt open source: the comparison that runs three years out
  • Total cost of ownership: licence, integration, operation and the exit
  • Vendor lock-in as a risk with a price, not as a slogan
  • The engineering budget: cloud, tooling, licences and people
  • FinOps for a lead: unit cost, showback and the bill nobody reads
  • Prioritising when everything is a priority: cost of delay and opportunity cost
  • Platform teams and golden paths: the standard that gets adopted because it is easier
  • The internal tool nobody uses, and why it was built anyway
  • Technical decisions that are product decisions wearing a disguise
  • Architecture decision records as the team’s memory
  • Working with product: discovery, trade-offs and a roadmap with two authors
  • Saying no: the alternative, the cost and the date
  • Presenting to executives: risk, money, and one slide
