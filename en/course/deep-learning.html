Deep Learning: Neural Networks in Practice

The part of machine learning that eats data and GPU hours: what a network actually learns, how to train one without losing a week, and when a boosting model would have been the better answer.

  • 70 hours
  • Level: advanced

What you need first

Requires machine learning: the split, the metrics and overfitting are taken as read. Linear algebra and derivatives show up, explained where they are used.

What you will learn

  • From the perceptron to backpropagation, with the maths that matters
  • Training: loss, optimiser, learning rate and overfitting
  • PyTorch: tensors, autograd and the training loop
  • Convolutional networks for images
  • Sequences, attention and transformers
  • Transfer learning, fine-tuning, and when not to use any of this

Full topic list

  • The perceptron, activation functions, and what a layer actually computes
  • Forward pass, loss and gradient descent in one dimension
  • Backpropagation, without hand-waving
  • Loss functions: MSE, cross-entropy, and the one that fits the task
  • Optimisers: SGD, momentum, Adam, and the learning rate above all
  • Batch size, epochs, and reading a training curve properly
  • Overfitting: regularisation, dropout, early stopping and augmentation
  • Normalisation: batch, layer, and why it makes training stable
  • PyTorch: tensors, autograd, modules and the training loop
  • Datasets and dataloaders, and the GPU sitting idle waiting for data
  • Convolution: filters, stride, padding and pooling
  • The classic architectures: ResNet, EfficientNet, and what each one changed
  • Augmentation and transfer learning for images
  • Sequences: RNN and LSTM, and why they were replaced
  • Attention and the transformer, from the inside
  • Embeddings, tokens, and what the model reads instead of text
  • Fine-tuning a pretrained model, and which layers to freeze
  • Tracking experiments: runs, seeds, and knowing what made the difference
  • Cost: GPU, mixed precision, and training time against the gain
  • When not to use deep learning: tabular data, little data, and the boosting baseline
