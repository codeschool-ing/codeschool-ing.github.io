Course

Data Visualisation

The right chart makes the data speak; the wrong one lies without meaning to. Learn to choose, draw and not mislead.

  • 60 hours
  • Level: intermediate

What you need first

Requires clean data to draw. No programming required: the principles hold in any tool.

What you will learn

  • Fundamentals: what the eye understands quickly
  • Chart families and when to use each one
  • Colour theory applied to data
  • Accessibility and contrast
  • Charts that mislead — and how not to make them
  • Responsive dashboards and reading on a phone

Full topic list

  • Visual encoding: position, length, angle, area, colour and shape
  • The hierarchy of accuracy: why bars beat pies
  • Bar and column charts: ordering and a baseline at zero
  • Line charts: trend, scale and dual axes (and why to avoid them)
  • Histograms and distribution: choosing the bin
  • Scatterplots, bubbles and trend lines
  • Heatmaps and correlation matrices
  • Geographic maps: choropleth, symbols and projections
  • Boxplots, violin plots and distribution charts
  • Small multiples instead of one overloaded chart
  • Colour theory: hue, saturation and lightness
  • Categorical, sequential and diverging palettes
  • Meaningful colour and decorative colour: the difference
  • Accessibility: colour blindness, contrast and never relying on colour alone
  • Labels, legends, titles that make a claim, and annotations
  • Charts that mislead: truncated axes, dual scales, wrong proportional area
  • Data-ink ratio and removing visual noise
  • Dashboard design: hierarchy, grouping and the path of the eye
  • Responsiveness: the dashboard on the director’s phone
  • Tools: Excel, Power BI, Tableau and code libraries
